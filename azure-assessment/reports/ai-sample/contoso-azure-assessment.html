1. Network topology

Pattern identified Multi-region hub-and-spoke with exceptions High confidence

The network topology at Contoso follows a multi-region hub-and-spoke pattern, with high confidence in the evidence provided. This design centralises shared services such as Azure Bastion, Firewall, and VPN gateway within regional hubs (vnet-hub-eastus and vnet-hub-westeurope), facilitating centralized management and security inspection. However, there are notable deviations from the Microsoft Cloud Adoption Framework's landing zone and Well-Architected Framework recommendations.

Direct peerings between spokes, such as those between vnet-prd-eastus and vnet-dev-eastus, bypass the central firewall, allowing un-inspected outbound traffic. Additionally, vnet-dev-sandbox is not peered to any network, creating an isolated environment that may expose it to unnecessary public access. These gaps could potentially undermine the security benefits of a hub-and-spoke architecture by allowing workloads in different regions and environments to communicate without central inspection or management.

To align with best practices, direct spoke-to-spoke peerings should be removed, routing all traffic through the hubs for consistent inspection. Furthermore, ensuring that vnet-dev-eastus routes 0.0.0.0/0 to the hub firewall will enforce comprehensive security measures on all outbound traffic. Lastly, a decision must be made regarding whether to integrate vnet-dev-sandbox into the main network or decommission it, as unmanaged networks can introduce additional risk and complexity.

2. Hybrid connectivity

Pattern identified Site-to-site VPN to on-premises High confidence

The current architecture employs a site-to-site VPN to connect on-premises infrastructure, adhering to the recommended shared-gateway design. The evidence is high, as indicated by the presence of vgw-hub-eastus, which is configured with a VpnGw2AZ SKU and operates in an active-standby mode within vnet-hub-eastus. This setup ensures redundancy and reliability for the connection.

However, there are gaps when compared to the Microsoft reference architecture. The current design only includes a gateway in the eastus region, while spokes in westeurope rely on cross-region connectivity through the hub gateway. If the eastus region becomes unavailable, this could disrupt on-premises connectivity. To mitigate this risk, it is recommended to deploy additional gateways in each regional hub, ensuring that all regions can independently establish a connection to on-premises sites.

Additionally, the current setup relies solely on IPsec tunnels over the Internet for production workloads, which may introduce latency and bandwidth limitations. For critical applications, leveraging ExpressRoute with a VPN as a backup could provide more robust connectivity options, enhancing both performance and reliability. Configuring the gateways in an active-active mode would also reduce the risk of single points of failure by ensuring that two on-premises devices can maintain the connection simultaneously.

In practice, these gaps imply potential vulnerabilities to regional outages and limitations in network performance for mission-critical workloads. Addressing these issues through additional gateway deployments and enhanced connectivity options will strengthen the overall hybrid environment's resilience and efficiency.
